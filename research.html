Research

When You Null, You Know: Naturalness Judgments in Mexican Spanish Cataphora

Departmental Honors Thesis
Supervisor: Jack Duff

My honors thesis examined how native Mexican Spanish speakers interpret null and overt pronouns in cataphoric constructions.

The study investigated whether null and overt pronouns show a division of labor and whether speakers prefer potential antecedents that match the grammatical features of the pronoun.

Participants completed a naturalness-judgment experiment in which pronoun type and subject compatibility were manipulated. The results showed a significant effect of subject compatibility, but no significant effect of pronoun type and no interaction between the two factors.

These findings suggest that speakers were sensitive to compatibility between the pronoun and its potential antecedent, but did not show a clear offline distinction between null and overt pronouns.

Methods: Experimental design, naturalness judgments, PCIbex, Prolific, R, and cumulative link mixed-effects modeling
