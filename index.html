I am a graduate of the UCLA Department of Linguistics , where I earned a B.A. in Linguistics and Psychology with College Honors and Departmental Honors. My research focuses on psycholinguistics, formal semantics, and sentence processing. I plan to apply to Ph.D. programs in Linguistics in Fall 2027.

My interests are broadly in psycholinguistics, with connections to formal semantics, particularly degree semantics, and the syntax-semantics interface . I am especially interested in how comprehenders resolve ambiguity in real time, which linguistic cues they use, and how they retrieve linguistic representations during processing . I am also interested in bilingualism and heritage languages as domains in which these questions can be explored. More broadly, I hope to connect experimental work with current theoretical frameworks in linguistics.

My departmental honors thesis, “When You Null, You Know: Naturalness Judgments in Mexican Spanish Cataphora,” was completed under the supervision of Jack Duff. The project examined the interpretation of null and overt pronouns in Spanish cataphoric constructions and investigated whether native Mexican Spanish speakers demonstrate a division of labor between the two pronoun types.

Research Interests: Psycholinguistics, formal semantics, sentence processing, anaphora and cataphora, degree semantics, Spanish linguistics, bilingualism, and linguistic fieldwork
